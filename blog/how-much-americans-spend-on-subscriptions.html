I built SubCheck because I got tired of guessing. Every month I'd look at my bank statement and think "wait, I'm still paying for that?" Turns out I'm not the only one. "How much do I spend on subscriptions" is one of the most searched personal finance questions on Google.

And the answer, for most of us, is "more than you think." Not because we're bad with money. Because subscriptions are specifically designed to fly under the radar. $12.99 here. $9.99 there. None of them feel like much on their own. But pull them all into one list and the number hits different.

Most of us can rattle off our top three or four subscriptions without thinking. It's the rest that get you. The free trial you forgot to cancel. The app you used twice last January. The streaming service you keep "just in case" something good drops. Those are the ones that quietly drain your account month after month.

The Ones You Forgot About

According to recent research, Americans waste close to $200 a year on subscriptions they don't even use. That's real money going to services that are just... sitting there.

And it's not an accident. These companies make it easy to sign up and hard to leave. Auto-renewal is on by default. Cancellation pages are buried three menus deep. The monthly charge is just small enough that you don't notice it on your statement. It's a system built to keep you paying, and it works.

5 Things That Actually Help

1

Figure out your real number

You can't fix what you can't see. Sit down for 10 minutes and list every single recurring charge. Every one. You'll almost certainly find something you forgot about.

2

Ask yourself one question per service

"Did I use this in the last 30 days?" If the answer is no, cancel it. You can always re-subscribe later if you miss it. (Spoiler: you probably won't.)

3

Stop paying for 5 streaming services at once

Pick one or two. Watch what you want. Cancel. Subscribe to the next one. Rotate every month or two. You'll watch the same amount of stuff and spend way less.

4

Go annual on the keepers

For the services you genuinely use every week, switch to yearly billing. Most offer a 15-20% discount for paying upfront. On 5 subscriptions, that's a few hundred bucks back in your pocket.

5

Put a reminder in your calendar

Set a recurring reminder every 3 months: "Review subscriptions." Takes 10 minutes. What you need changes over time, and a quick check keeps things from piling up again.

Here's the Thing

Subscriptions aren't bad. Paying for things you don't use is bad. The average American spends somewhere between $1,080 and $2,600 a year on recurring services, and almost everyone underestimates their own number by about half.

Just knowing what you spend changes how you think about it. Once you see the real total, you'll know exactly what to keep and what to cut. It's not about being cheap. It's about spending on the stuff you actually care about.
