I'm Spending $313/Month on Subscriptions — Here's What I Learned

A subscription audit revealed $3,756 in annual spending. The real waste wasn't forgotten services—it was nice-to-have subscriptions I kept out of habit.

The Reality Check

After building SubCheck, I used it on myself. Total monthly subscription spending: $313, or $3,756 annually.

I categorized everything into three groups: Essential (daily use, can't replace), Nice-to-Have (valuable but not critical), and Cancel-Worthy (paying for but not using).

The breakdown:

  • Essential: $149/month (Claude Pro, Apple One, Netflix, Tennis Channel, iCloud storage)
  • Nice-to-Have: $142/month (MyFICO, AMC A-List, streaming services, Masterclass)
  • Cancel-Worthy: $22/month (duplicate ChatGPT subscription, STARZ)

What I Learned

Annual billing hides costs. Six subscriptions bill annually, totaling $24/month in monthly equivalents. But they appear as lump sums once per year—$117 here, $92 there—making them invisible in monthly budget reviews.

The real problem isn't waste, it's drift. Only $22/month was pure waste (duplicate subscriptions, unused services). The real opportunity was $142/month in "nice-to-have" spending—services I kept out of habit rather than active use.

Duplicates happen even when you're paying attention. Two ChatGPT Plus subscriptions on different email accounts cost me $20/month for six months before I noticed. Not a forgotten trial—just administrative oversight.

Seasonal services cost more as year-round subscriptions. HBO Max and AMC A-List usage varies by season. Converting these to on-demand (subscribe when needed, cancel when not) saves $250+ annually without losing value.

The Fix

Immediate cuts ($42/month): Duplicate ChatGPT subscription, STARZ, redundant OpenAI subscription.

Downgrades ($30/month): Replace MyFICO with free credit monitoring from banks or Credit Karma.

Seasonal rotation (~$250/year): Cancel HBO Max and AMC A-List off-season, reactivate when usage justifies it.

Result: $313/month → $241/month. Annual savings: $864.

The remaining $241 represents intentional spending on actively-used services, not passive accumulation of forgotten charges.

If You're Doing This Yourself

Spend one hour listing every subscription. Focus on:

  • Annual charges — Convert to monthly equivalents to see real recurring costs
  • "Nice-to-have" category — Services you use occasionally but could eliminate without real impact
  • Seasonal patterns — Services used 3-4 months per year but paid for 12

The goal isn't minimizing spending—it's aligning dollars with actual value received.
