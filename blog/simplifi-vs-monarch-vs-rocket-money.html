Simplifi vs Monarch vs Rocket Money: Which Subscription Tracker Is Worth It?

Four paid tools promise to find your subscriptions and keep them under control. They differ in price, in how much automation you get, and in one thing most reviews bury: three of the four work by connecting to your bank accounts, and the fourth, YNAB, pointedly does not have to. Here is the honest comparison, including the free option.

By Ron Satha · Last updated November 3, 2026 · Prices checked on each company's own site on November 3, 2026

Each of these tools attacks the same problem from a different angle. Quicken Simplifi and Monarch are full personal finance dashboards that track subscriptions as part of a bigger picture. Rocket Money leads with finding and cancelling subscriptions. YNAB is a budgeting method that happens to keep your recurring commitments in view. Three of them find recurring charges by looking at your transactions, which means connecting your bank and card accounts. YNAB is the exception: it imports transactions too, but manual entry and file-based import are first-class ways to use it. That connection is the real price of admission for the other three, and it is worth deciding how you feel about it before you compare monthly fees.

Quicken Simplifi: The Full Budget App at the Lowest Price

Who it fits: Simplifi is the cheapest complete budgeting app in this group, and it does not feel stripped down for it. Its Spending Plan starts with your income, subtracts your bills and subscriptions, and shows what is left to spend. It tracks upcoming bills and subscriptions, projects your cash flow, and adds investment tracking and customizable reports. It connects to more than 14,000 financial institutions, so most banks and cards are covered. If you want one app quietly keeping score of everything, this is the value pick.

Who it does not fit: Anyone who wants to try before paying. There is no free trial, and the 30-day money-back guarantee only applies to subscriptions bought directly from Quicken, not through an app store. It also will not cancel anything for you: it shows you the subscriptions, and the cancelling is still your job. And like Monarch and Rocket Money, the automation depends on connecting your accounts.

Monarch: The All-in-One Dashboard

Who it fits: Monarch is the pick for people, and especially households, who want their whole financial life in one place: budgets, cash flow, net worth, investments, and goals in a single dashboard, with recurring charges flagged along the way. Core costs $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year, and the first week is free. A Plus tier adds forecasting tools, tracking for multiple income sources such as a small business, and deeper investment analysis. Monarch also offers a money-back guarantee, which softens the no-free-plan problem.

Who it does not fit: Anyone whose only problem is subscriptions. You would be paying dashboard prices to solve a list problem. There is no free tier at all, so the trial week is your only no-cost look. And despite the polish, Monarch will not cancel a subscription for you.

Rocket Money: The Subscription Hunter

Who it fits: Rocket Money is built around the exact job this post is about. Link your accounts and it surfaces your recurring charges; the free tier already tracks subscriptions and shows your spending, which makes it the easiest of the four to test. It is also the only one that will cancel subscriptions for you: on Premium, its team cancels many unwanted subscriptions, usually within 2 to 10 days. Premium uses a pay what you think is fair model, typically $7 to $14 a month after a 7-day free trial, and Premium+ at $15 a month adds an AI assistant. Its other headline service is bill negotiation: where it has been offered, a successful negotiation cost 35 to 60 percent of the first year's savings, and as of November 2026 Rocket Money has been moving bill negotiation into Premium+, where members get it with no success fee instead.

Who it does not fit: Anyone who does not want to link bank accounts, because scanning linked accounts is the whole mechanism. Free users also give up web access, which is a Premium feature, and give up the cancellation service. And its budgeting is fine rather than deep: if budgeting is your main goal, Simplifi and YNAB both do more.

YNAB: The Method, Not the Dashboard

Who it fits: YNAB is the odd one out, in a good way. It is a zero-based budgeting method: you give every dollar a job before you spend it, and the software exists to keep you honest about that plan. It has the longest trial here at 34 days, it does not ask for a credit card when you sign up on its website, and one subscription can be shared with up to six people. It is also the only one of the four that works fully without a bank connection, because manual entry and file-based import are treated as first-class options rather than leftovers. College students can even get a free 365-day trial.

Who it does not fit: Subscription hunters. YNAB keeps recurring payments visible as scheduled transactions, but it is not designed to dig forgotten subscriptions out of your statements, and it will not cancel anything. At $109 a year it is also the highest annual price in this group, and the method asks for regular attention: check in weekly or the plan quietly stops matching your life.

When the Free Manual Audit Is Enough

Here is the part most comparison posts skip. If your subscription list is short enough to write down, if your charges sit on one or two cards, or if you simply do not want to give any company ongoing access to your bank transactions, the paid automation may be more than you need. The core job is small: list every recurring charge, convert annual bills into monthly costs so nothing hides, total it up, and decide what stays. That is exactly what the free SubCheck calculator does, in your browser, with no account and no bank login: run a manual audit.

The paid tools earn their fee when the picture is bigger: charges spread across many accounts, a budget you want maintained every month, a partner who needs the same dashboard, or a stack of cancellations you would rather hand to Rocket Money's team. If none of that sounds like you, run the free audit first and keep the money. You can always subscribe to a tracker later. Ideally, pick one you will not forget to cancel.

The Short Version

  • Best value full app: Simplifi, at $6.99 a month billed annually
  • Best all-in-one dashboard: Monarch, especially for households
  • Best for subscription cleanup specifically: Rocket Money, the only one that cancels for you
  • Best if you want new money habits: YNAB, and it is the only one that does not require linking your bank
  • Best if you want no account, no bank link, and no new subscription: the free manual audit below
